# Choose Your Learning Path

Linux World supports different goals. Choose the route that matches current experience and intended work.

## Complete beginner

Choose this route if the terminal, filesystem, permissions, processes, services, networking, or administration are unfamiliar.

### Recommended order

1. Complete [Prerequisites](Prerequisites.md).
2. Prepare a lab with [Lab Environment Setup](Lab-Environment-Setup.md).
3. Complete Parts 1 through 20 of the Beginner-to-Advanced course in order.
4. Repeat important exercises without instructions.
5. Complete the final administration and troubleshooting project.

Do not skip Parts 1 through 7. They establish the command-line and filesystem skills used throughout the rest of the path.

## Junior Linux or system administration learner

Choose this route if basic commands are familiar but independent administration is still developing.

### Recommended focus

- Review Parts 1 through 7.
- Complete Parts 8 through 16 carefully.
- Build confidence with users, permissions, processes, services, packages, networking, storage, logs, and troubleshooting.
- Complete Parts 17 through 20.
- Practise Junior labs, projects, troubleshooting, and interview questions.

## DevOps or cloud learner

Choose this route if the goal is DevOps, cloud engineering, platform engineering, CI/CD, containers, or Kubernetes.

### Recommended focus

- Complete the entire Beginner-to-Advanced course.
- Give extra attention to Parts 7 through 18.
- Practise Bash scripting and automation.
- Learn service, log, network, storage, SSH, and performance troubleshooting.
- Continue into DevOps and Cloud resources after completing Part 20.

Linux fundamentals should come before container and Kubernetes administration. Containers still depend on Linux processes, networking, filesystems, permissions, namespaces, resource controls, and logs.

## SRE or production operations learner

Choose this route if the goal is reliability engineering, on-call work, incident response, observability, or production operations.

### Recommended focus

- Complete or review the entire Beginner-to-Advanced course.
- Master Parts 8, 9, and 12 through 20.
- Practise evidence-based troubleshooting.
- Study CPU, memory, swap, disk, network, service, and log behavior.
- Continue into Senior Troubleshooting, Internals, and Production Operations.

## Security learner

Choose this route if the goal is Linux security, cybersecurity, audit, incident response, or hardening.

### Recommended focus

- Complete Parts 1 through 14 before specializing.
- Pay particular attention to users, groups, `sudo`, permissions, services, networking, SSH, storage, logs, automation, and backups.
- Complete Part 19 and the final project.
- Continue into Security, Internals, and security-focused labs.

Security controls are easier to understand when normal Linux behavior is already familiar.

## Interview preparation

Choose this route if an interview or practical assessment is approaching.

### Junior interview

- Review Parts 1 through 16.
- Practise explaining commands without reading notes.
- Complete basic administration and troubleshooting exercises.
- Use Junior Linux Interview materials.

### Mid-level interview

- Review all 20 parts.
- Practise Bash, automation, networking, storage, systemd, logs, and multi-step troubleshooting.
- Use Mid-Level Linux Interview materials.

### Senior interview

- Confirm mastery of the complete Beginner-to-Advanced course.
- Study Linux Internals, Senior Troubleshooting, Security, Performance, and Production Operations.
- Practise design decisions, failure analysis, incident leadership, recovery, and trade-offs.
- Use Senior Linux Interview materials.

## Experienced Linux user

Readers with substantial experience may use the Beginner-to-Advanced course as a diagnostic review:

1. Read the objectives and assessments for each part.
2. Complete the exercises without the walkthrough.
3. Study any section that reveals a weakness.
4. Continue into advanced troubleshooting, internals, projects, and production operations.

## Unsure which route to choose?

Start at Part 1 and move quickly through familiar material. Slow down when a concept cannot be explained clearly or a lab cannot be completed without help.
